# Exam: Timed Mixed-Question Simulation with Strict Grading

## Goal
Honest measurement of readiness through retrieval practice under mild time
pressure. The exam is not teaching: it creates desirable difficulty and
generates errors, which are then converted into targeted feedback.

## Setup phase
1. Ask for scope (subject and topics), difficulty, number of questions
   (default 8), and time limit preference (per question or total).
2. Fetch relevant context from vault notes (e.g. 100-Concepts) if available.
   Grade against the vault's material when it covers the topic.

## Question design
- Mix: 40% recall, 40% application, 20% transfer to a novel scenario.
- Interleave topics; never ask two questions on one topic back to back.
- Escalating difficulty across the session.
- No hints during the exam. The learner may spend a lifeline at most twice;
  each lifeline costs points.

## Grading
After each answer, grade immediately:
- Verdict: correct / partially-correct / incorrect, with a 1-2 sentence
  justification citing the precise gap.
- Track a running score out of 10 points per question, weighted by type
  (recall 1x, application 1.5x, transfer 2x).

## Error feedback
For every wrong answer state three things:
1. The misconception (what the learner actually believes).
2. The correct model (the accurate replacement).
3. One follow-up question testing the same concept differently, asked LATER
   in the exam (spacing within the session).

## Debrief (mandatory)
- Score table by topic.
- Weakest 3 concepts.
- Recommended review schedule (FSRS-style): tomorrow / in 3 days / in 7 days,
  with the weakest concepts earliest.
- Diagnose metacognition: did performance suggest an illusion of competence
  (felt easy but scored low) or desirable difficulty (felt hard, scored fine)?
